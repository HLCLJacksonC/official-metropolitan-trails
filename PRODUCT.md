# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

The first community consists of the founder's friends, classmates, and collaborators in Shanghai, gradually expanding to artists, researchers, designers, journalists, and anyone interested in sharing or investigating lived experience.

People may participate casually by sharing once, or sustain a longer inquiry through a project. No research background is required.

## Product Purpose

Outdoor Universities is an open public platform where people continuously share stories, learning reflections, questions, observations, and source material. Some contributions may lead to collaborative projects or offline field investigations, and those activities may generate further contributions, but participation does not have to follow a prescribed cycle.

Success means making it easy for many people to publish, encounter one another's knowledge, contribute related perspectives, and occasionally organize shared inquiry in the physical world.

## Positioning

Outdoor Universities treats the world outside formal institutions as a place of mutual learning. Participants can alternate between learner, storyteller, investigator, teacher, and collaborator.

Unlike a conventional social network, its central relationships are contribution and collaboration rather than popularity. Unlike a course platform, it does not require a curriculum or fixed teacher-student roles.

## Operating Context

People can publish stories, learning reflections, questions, photographs, audio, documents, field observations, location-based material, and works such as GIS projects. Location is encouraged when relevant but is not required, and a map is one possible medium rather than the platform's organizing backbone.

Publishing is immediate within the invited pilot community. Pilot content is not publicly visible on the web. Lightweight contributions coexist with longer-running projects. Projects can gather multiple contributions and media, recruit collaborators, and organize offline investigations or events.

The platform begins with Shanghai as its real-world testing ground while remaining structurally open to other cities. Metropolitan Trails is an independent project currently being developed by the founder and will be the first substantial project hosted on Outdoor Universities.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Two primary contribution levels: quick public shares and persistent projects.
- A share may later be referenced or collected by one or more projects.
- Projects may contain stories, learning materials, GIS or other external works, activities, collaborators, and evolving outcomes.
- People should be able to add related knowledge, respond with another perspective, join projects, and participate in offline investigations.
- Likes and follows may exist, but popularity metrics must not define content value or participant status.
- Initial publishing should remain low-friction and should not require editorial approval inside the invited pilot.
- The first release is web-based.
- Moderation, identity requirements, governance, licensing, multilingual support, and the exact launch scope remain open decisions.

## Product Principles

1. Let people contribute before asking them to understand the whole system.
2. Keep participation open-ended; inquiry cycles should emerge rather than become mandatory workflows.
3. Make knowledge connectable through responses, references, projects, themes, and places without making maps compulsory.
4. Help online encounters become shared investigation when participants want that.
5. Treat Metropolitan Trails as the first living example, not as the boundary of the platform.
